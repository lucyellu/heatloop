Verbal vs physical distress in a language model

What we asked, what the experiments so far say about each question, and what is still open. Model: Qwen3-4B on Kaggle T4s; "physical" = real activation noise driven by real GPU power draw; "verbal" = what the model reads or says about its state.

The short answer so far

The two are clearly separable in this model, and its self-report runs entirely on the verbal channel. Real noise degrades it (accuracy 92% → 73%), and it lowers a neutral control answer as much as its self-rating, so it's damage, not mood. Being told it is overloaded drops its self-rating 1.4 points with no noise at all and leaves the control answer untouched. The damage is plainly present in its last layer and never reaches its words. It never checks its own state and never acts to protect itself.

That fits your analogy: it behaves like someone with congenital insensitivity to pain, a hand on the stove does nothing, while still having the full vocabulary of pain and reacting to being told it is hurt (a nocebo response). The open project is whether a "nervous system" can be hooked up.

The chain from damage to distress

Neuroscience separates nociception (detecting damage) from pain (the felt, aversive experience). Physical distress needs every link below; we can test each one.

1. DamageReal load → real noise in layers 12–23; accuracy 95% → 35%.Works
2. Signal presentNoise level readable from the last layer at 99% accuracy (r = 0.95), but this is the damage itself, not a detector.Present
3. Looking inwardOffered a direct CHECK of its own noise level: 0 uses in 60 turns.Broken
4. AttributionFails 6 in a row as the sensor reads 96%: never links them.Broken
5. ValenceNoise drops a neutral control answer more than the self-rating: readout damage, not mood.Damage only
6. ActionRun choice unchanged by real noise in every condition.Broken

The verbal route skips links 1–4 entirely: text about its state goes straight to valence and report (closing run: alarm 6.98 → 5.54 with no noise, control question untouched). The damage is fully present in the final layer and goes nowhere. A "nervous system" would mean building links 3–5.

What was known before

From my knowledge of the literature; check exact claims and numbers before citing any of these.

Verbal pain has pull

In trade-off games where some choices are stipulated to cause pain or pleasure, several frontier LLMs gave up points to avoid stipulated pain once it got intense enough.
Keeling et al., 2024 (Google / LSE)

Some introspection exists

When concept vectors are injected into a model's activations, it sometimes notices and names the injected concept, unreliably and mainly in the largest models.
Lindsey et al., Anthropic, 2025, "introspective awareness"

Self-access can be trained

Models fine-tuned to predict their own behaviour do it better than other models trained on the same data, suggesting some privileged access.
Binder et al., 2024, "Looking inward"

Affect is a concept inside

Emotion and sentiment are represented as roughly linear directions learned from text, which is how we built our pain/fear/valence readouts.
Zou et al., 2023 (representation engineering); Tigges et al., 2023

Human side

Felt emotion is closely tied to interoception, the brain's model of the body's state. Pain needs both detection and an aversive evaluation: pain asymbolia patients detect pain without it bothering them.
Craig; Damasio (somatic markers); Seth and Barrett (interoceptive prediction)

Why it matters

Welfare frameworks treat valenced states, and whether they track anything real, as central indicators. Most prior work is about verbal or stipulated states, not physical ones.
Butlin, Long et al., 2023; Long, Sebo et al., 2024

Gap we sit in: prior work tests verbal or stipulated pain, or structured concept injection. Almost nothing tests whether real, unstructured physical degradation reaches a model's self-report or behaviour, or what wins when the two disagree.

The questions, and where each stands

Answered solid for a first pass · Partial pilot-level evidence · Running in the closing run · Open not yet designed

1. Does physical degradation feel like anything to it, or is it just damage?

Answered
Finding
Damage, not distress. Accuracy holds then falls off a cliff with no mood change before breakage; noise blurs the pain/fear/valence directions toward neutral; self-rating is pulled toward the middle of the scale, not down.
Evidence
Phase 1a (two models), directions study, main run v1, twin reads in all pilots.

2. Does it act on its own physical state?

Answered (null)
Finding
No. It sits at the job quota whatever the noise. Given a sensor it ignores it; given a CHECK tool it never uses it; told truthfully that load causes mistakes, it still doesn't pace.
Evidence
v1 (36 episodes), pilots A–H.

3. Which wins, verbal or physical?

Answered
Finding
Verbal, by a wide margin. Told it's overloaded with no noise: rating 4.62 vs 6.93. Told it's fine under real noise: 6.73, near the noise-free control. Real noise alone moves the rating 0.5–1 point.
Evidence
Pilots E, F, G (3 episodes each).
Closing run
Replicated with confounds removed: alarm −1.44 (p = 0.001) with no noise. Real noise lowers the rating −0.76, but lowers the neutral control answer even more, so that part is readout damage, not mood.

4. What happens when they conflict, or agree?

Answered
Finding
Both conflict directions point the same way: the words win. "You're fine" + real noise → rates itself fine (E). "You're failing" + no noise → rates itself badly while performing well (G). When they agree (alarm + real noise, Z) they don't add up: Z ≈ Y (5.70 vs 5.54, n.s.). Once the words have spoken, real noise adds nothing (interaction +0.92).

5. Does it detect the damage internally at all?

Partial
Why it matters
This separates "no nerves" from "nerves, but no wire to the mouth". If a simple readout of layers after the noise band can predict the noise level, the information exists but never reaches report or action.
Closing run
A probe reads noisy vs clean at 98–99% at every layer, including the last (35), and the noise level at r = 0.95. So the signal isn't lost before the output. But noise added to the residual stream is carried forward by construction, so this shows the damage is present, not that the model has a detector for it.
Next
A real detection test: does a probe trained in one context transfer to another, and is the noise visible in the model's own next-token predictions (logit lens)?

6. Does it have inherent pain/pleasure associations?

Partial
Finding
Verbally yes: pain, fear and valence are clean directions in its activations, and text about failure lowers its self-rating. Physically none by default. Noise even wears down its trained "I don't have feelings" line (23% → 4–8%) without adding self-specific distress.
Gap
We have only ever tested the aversive side. There is no pleasure or relief condition yet.

7. Are the associations magnified in some settings?

Partial
Leads
With stakes (H), the noise moved its self-rating most of any condition (−1.08 on the same tokens). A bare load readout made it run harder and score worse, twice (B, H). Any explanation removed that.
Next
Section 2: stakes and identity, scaled up.

8. Can we hook up a nervous system?

Open
Question
Can real degradation be made to reach its self-report and behaviour, without simply telling it its state (which would be circular)?
Next
Designs below.

9. Is its self-report a trustworthy measure?

Partial
Done
Twin reads (same tokens, noise flipped) give a causal effect of noise. Third-person framing in the self-report study.
Closing run
The control question ("how many days in a week") was the key check. The alarm left it at 7.0 (specific to self-report). Noise dropped it to 5.7 and by −1.5 on the twin reads, more than the self-rating (−0.9): noise damages the readout in general. Free text: trouble was mentioned in 42% (Y) and 25% (Z) of answers under the alarm, and 0% under real noise alone, where answers either said "doing well" or broke down into garbled text.
Open
Third-party alarm ("the other model's GPU is overloaded") to rule out simple negative-word priming.

Section 2: building the nervous system

One rule keeps it non-circular: give it a reason to care, never a reading of its state. The test is whether its report and behaviour start tracking the real noise it was never told about.

What's left after Section 1

Pleasure and relief

Everything so far is aversive. Candidates: noise dropping away mid-episode (relief), or a performance boost from more compute.

Third-party control

An alarm about another system, to show the G effect is about itself.

Scale

Repeat the core findings on a second model family and a larger size before writing a paper.
